# 2.1 Fades, grid and snap

Screenshots: `app-fades-grid.png` (fades, a crossfade, volume lines and a
half-beat grid across a tempo change), `app-fades-window.png` (the Fades
and volume window) and `app-grid-auto.png` (the grid following the zoom).

## On the PC

- [ ] **2.1 Fades you can hear.** Import a sustained sound (a strummed
      chord held out). Hover over the clip: a small square appears at
      each top corner. Drag the left one right by a beat, and the right
      one left by two beats. Pass: the waveform tapers under each fade, a
      curve shows its shape, and playback swells in and dies away with no
      click at either end. The same in **File → Export**.
- [ ] **2.1 Fade shapes.** Right-click the clip → **Fade in shape**, and
      try each of Straight, Smooth (steady power), S-curve, Fast start and
      Slow start. Pass: the curve on the clip changes, and you can hear
      the difference on a slow fade (Fast start gets loud sooner, Slow
      start later).
- [ ] **2.1 Crossfades.** Split a clip (S), drag the second half a little
      left so it overlaps the first, select both and press **X** (or
      **Crossfade**). Pass: the join plays smoothly with no click and no
      dip or bump in level. Also: two clips that just touch (end to
      start) get a short 20 ms crossfade when you press X. Say if you'd
      like crossfades made by themselves whenever clips overlap.
- [ ] **2.1 Clip volume.** Tick **Volume** in the timeline toolbar. Drag
      a clip's yellow line down. Pass: the clip gets quieter, its name
      shows the level (for example "−6.0 dB"), and the waveform shrinks
      to match. Double-click the line to add points and drag them to make
      a dip in the middle of the clip. Pass: you hear the dip, and
      right-click on a point → **Delete volume point** removes it.
- [ ] **2.1 Fades and volume by number.** Select some clips and press
      **F** (or **Fades…**). Type 250 into Fade in. Pass: every selected
      clip fades in over 250 ms. A screen reader reads the window's
      controls by name, and a clip with fades is read as, for example,
      "Riff on Guitar, bar 1 to bar 3, fades in over 250 ms".
- [ ] **2.1 The grid.** Choose ½ beat, ⅓ beat (triplets), ¼ beat and so
      on from the grid list next to **Snap**. Pass: lines appear inside
      each bar, beats stronger than their parts, and dragged clips and
      trims land on them. Add a tempo change: the lines spread out or
      close up after it. Choose **Auto** and zoom in and out: the list
      shows which step it is using and the lines get finer as you zoom
      in.
- [ ] **2.1 Snap on and off, and snapping to clips.** Untick **Snap** (or
      press **N**): clips drag freely. Tick **To clips**: a clip dragged
      near another clip's start or end, a marker or the playhead jumps to
      it. Holding **Alt** while dragging still places freely.
- [ ] **2.1 Nudge.** Click a clip so it has the keyboard, and press Left
      and Right. Pass: it moves by one grid step (½ beat if that's the
      grid); Shift+Left/Right moves a bar.
- [ ] **2.1 Slip.** Trim a clip's start in a bit, then hold **Ctrl** and
      drag the middle of the clip. Pass: the clip stays where it is while
      the audio slides inside it, and it stops at the file's start and
      end.
- [ ] **2.1 Ripple.** Put three clips in a row on one track. Tick
      **Ripple**, select the middle one and press Delete. Pass: the third
      clip moves left to close the gap. Drag the first clip's end shorter:
      the clips after it follow. Untick Ripple: they stay put. Markers
      and other tracks never move.
- [ ] **2.1 Zoom.** Select a clip and press **Z** (or **Zoom to clips**).
      Pass: it fills the timeline. **=** and **−** zoom in and out. All
      of these are in the new **View** menu and in Settings → Key
      commands.
- [ ] **2.1 Saved, shared and undone.** Save, close and reopen; then open
      the project on the second computer. Pass: fades, shapes, volumes
      and volume points are all there. Edit → Undo takes back each fade
      or volume change. A frozen track (one with a plugin the other
      computer lacks) asks to be frozen again after a fade changes.

## Decisions to accept

- [ ] **Volume points stay with the audio.** Built: a clip's volume
      points are fixed to the recording, so trimming or slipping the clip
      carries them along. The fades, by contrast, stay at the clip's
      ends. Say if you'd rather points stayed at the same place on the
      timeline.
- [ ] **Overlapping clips still both play.** Built as before: where two
      clips on a track overlap, you hear both until you crossfade them.
      The usual alternative is that the clip on top silences the one
      under it. Say which you want.
- [ ] **Ctrl-drag slips.** Built: Ctrl held when you start dragging a
      clip's middle slips its audio (Ctrl-click still adds to the
      selection). Say if you'd like a different key.
- [ ] **Ripple moves one track only.** Built: ripple editing moves the
      later clips on the same track, not other tracks or markers.
