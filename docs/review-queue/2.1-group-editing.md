# 2.1 Group editing

Tracks can be grouped, such as the mics on a drum kit, so that their
clips are edited together and stay in time with each other.

## On the PC

- [ ] **2.1 Making a group.** Record (or import) the same few bars onto
      three tracks, say Kick, Snare and Overheads. Select a clip on each
      (Shift-click), then **Edit → Edit groups…** → **New group of the
      selected clips' tracks**. Pass: a group called "Group 1" appears
      with those three tracks ticked, and each track's name on the
      timeline shows "Group 1" under it. Rename it "Drums".
- [ ] **2.1 Selecting together.** Click the clip on Kick. Pass: the
      clips at the same time on Snare and Overheads are selected too.
      Shift-click it again: all three are deselected.
- [ ] **2.1 Editing together.** With the Drums clips selected, try each
      of: split at the playhead (S), drag the clips along, nudge with
      Left and Right, Delete (with Ripple on and off), crossfade (X)
      and Fades… (F). Pass: every edit happens on all three tracks at
      once, and Edit → Undo takes each one back in a single step.
- [ ] **2.1 Edges and fades together.** Drag the start edge of the Kick
      clip in by a beat, then its end edge, then its fade-in handle.
      Ctrl+Left/Right and Alt+Left/Right on the focused clip do the same
      from the keyboard. Pass: the Snare and Overheads clips are trimmed
      and faded by the same amount, so all three still line up.
- [ ] **2.1 Volume stays separate.** Tick **Volume** and drag the Kick
      clip's volume line down. Pass: only the Kick clip gets quieter.
- [ ] **2.1 Editing one track alone.** Untick **Groups** in the timeline
      toolbar (or press **G**), then move the Snare clip a little. Pass:
      only the Snare clip moves, and the group names under the tracks go
      faint. Tick it again and the group is back. Unticking **On** for
      a group in the Edit groups window does the same for just that
      group.
- [ ] **2.1 Saved and shared.** Save, close and reopen; then open the
      project on the second computer. Pass: the group, its name, its
      tracks and whether it is on are all kept. **Snapshots** lists
      group changes under "Groups".
- [ ] **2.1 Screen reader.** Tab to a grouped clip. Pass: it is read
      as, for example, "Take 1 on Kick (group Drums), bar 1 to bar 3".

## Decisions to accept

- [ ] **Which clips go together.** Built: a clip goes with the clips on
      the group's other tracks that overlap it in time (and, for
      recorded takes, the same take). Most tools match this way. The
      stricter choice is only clips that start and end at exactly the
      same place. Say if overlap pulls in clips you didn't expect.
- [ ] **Grouped clips stay on their tracks.** Built: while any selected
      clip is on a grouped track, dragging up or down (or Ctrl+Up/Down)
      doesn't move clips to another track, so each mic stays on its
      own. Turn group editing off (G) to move a clip to another track.
- [ ] **Volume isn't grouped.** Built: clip volume and volume points
      are always per clip, as each mic usually needs its own level.
      Say if you'd like a choice to change volume together.
- [ ] **One group per track.** Built: putting a track in a group takes
      it out of any other. Groups inside groups aren't offered.
- [ ] **Separate from the mixer.** Edit groups only affect editing on
      the timeline. Linking faders, mutes and solos of a set of tracks
      is part of mixer routing (control groups), built separately.
