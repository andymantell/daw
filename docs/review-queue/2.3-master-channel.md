# 2.3 Master channel

The mixer's Master row is now a channel: the whole mix goes through its
effects (after every track and bus) and then its fader. Assistants reach
it as the track `master`. Built in the cloud, so the mixer row and the
effects window are untested by hand.

- [ ] **2.3 Master fader.** Play a song and move **Master volume** in the
      mixer. Pass: the level follows at once with no click or drop-out,
      the master meter follows, and after saving and opening again the
      fader is where you left it.
- [ ] **2.3 Master effects.** Press the master row's **FX** and add a
      limiter, then an EQ before it. Pass: the window is titled
      "Effects: Master", the mix goes through them (set the limiter's
      ceiling low: the master meter stops there), the row's button reads
      "FX (2)", and a track's FX button closes the master's window and
      the other way round.
- [ ] **2.3 Undo and save.** Add an effect on the master, change its
      settings, undo twice. Pass: both steps come back off, and the
      history (Edit → Change history) names them under "Master". Save,
      open again: the chain and settings are back.
- [ ] **2.3 Latency.** Put a plugin with latency (a look-ahead limiter)
      on the master and a click track against a recorded click. Pass:
      playback stays in time with itself, and the headphone mixes
      (monitor section) stay in time with the main output.
- [ ] **2.3 Low-latency monitoring.** With the big mix buffer on
      (Settings → Audio device), arm a guitar track and play through it
      with a limiter on the master. Pass: the guitar is heard through the
      master's effects, in time with the backing, and the effect isn't
      lost when you arm or disarm tracks.
- [ ] **2.3 Export.** Export the mix with a limiter on the master and the
      master fader at -6 dB. Pass: the mix file has gone through both.
      Export stems and bus stems too. Pass: each stem has gone through
      the master too (a stem of a track playing alone matches the mix).
- [ ] **2.3 Master automation.** In the automation pane, pick **Master**
      in the track list (after the tracks) and add a Volume lane, then
      draw a fade out over the last bars. Pass: the song fades out, the
      master fader follows the lane while playing, and + Lane offers the
      master's volume and its effects' settings but no pan. Set the mode
      to Touch and move the master fader while playing. Pass: a lane is
      written and plays back after. Save, open again: the lane is back.
- [ ] **2.3 Render in place still before the master.** With a limiter on
      the master, render a track in place (or freeze it). Pass: the new
      audio has not been through the limiter (it plays back through the
      master, so it would otherwise go through it twice).

## Decisions to accept

- [x] **Stems go through the master.** Built (Andy asked): track stems
      and bus stems go through the master's effects, fader and
      automation, as the mix does. A compressor or limiter on the master
      reacts to each stem alone, so the stems won't sum exactly to the
      mix when one is there. Render in place and freezing stay before
      the master, because what they make plays back through it. An
      assistant's `analyse` of one track or bus is before the master
      too (it is a look at that track); `analyse` of the mix or of
      `master` is after it.
- [x] **Master automation.** Built (Andy asked): the master's fader and
      its effects' settings have lanes, in the same modes (Off, Read,
      Touch, Latch, Write) as tracks. Assistants use the track `master`
      in `get_automation`, `edit_automation` and `set_automation_mode`.
- [x] **No sidechain on the master.** A compressor on the master can't
      listen to a track. Left out: the use (ducking the whole mix under a
      voice-over or talkback) is rare in music, and a bus does it. Say if
      you want it.
