# 3.1 Note editing depth

Built at risk while the owner was away (plan item B1, the rest of
roadmap 3.1 after the notes foundation). The note editor gains a key
and scale, scale mode, humanise, legato, choosing notes by what they
are, controller lanes, per-note expression, groove templates from notes
or audio, and MIDI learn. Every tool is a command (the new **Notes**
menu, and Ctrl+K), so keys and anything driving the app from outside
reach the same thing; each acts on the note editor's selected notes, or
on all of them with none selected.

## On the PC

- [ ] **3.1 Key and scale mode.** Open a note clip in the note editor
      (key E). Pick a key in "Key" (say A → A minor). Pass: rows off
      the scale are shaded and the root's rows tinted; the key is the
      song's, and "Own key" gives just this clip one. Turn on "Scale
      mode": drawing a note on a shaded row puts it on the nearest
      scale note, and dragging notes up or down keeps them on it.
      "Fit to scale" moves notes already off it.
- [ ] **3.1 Humanise and legato.** Draw a stiff part, select some
      notes (or none for all) and press Humanise. Pass: they move a
      little in time, velocity and length, by at most the amounts
      beside the button; Undo puts them back. Legato makes each note
      last until the next starts (chords together), less the gap.
- [ ] **3.1 Select notes.** Notes → Select notes… (or "Select…").
      Tick ranges of bars, pitch, velocity, chance or length, or "Only
      notes off the scale". Pass: "Select" picks the matching notes; the
      mode choices add to or narrow the selection.
- [ ] **3.1 Controller lanes.** Choose "Lane" → Mod wheel (or Sustain
      pedal, Pitch wheel, Expression…, or Other controller). Click in
      the lane to add points, drag them, right-click to remove. Pass:
      the Simple synth follows the mod wheel (vibrato), volume (7),
      expression (11), brightness (74), the pedal and the pitch wheel;
      playing from the middle of the song starts with the value in force
      there; a MIDI output gets the controllers too. Lanes in use show a
      dot in the list.
- [ ] **3.1 Per-note expression.** Select one note, choose "Lane" →
      Note pitch (or pressure, timbre), and draw its line, or press
      "Wobble" for vibrato. Pass: the note bends, swells or brightens as
      it sounds, and notes with expression show a thin mark. A plugin
      instrument gets these once plugin instruments are in (plan B2); a
      MIDI output gets pressure only (key pressure), as plain MIDI has
      no message for a single note's pitch or timbre.
- [ ] **3.1 Grooves.** Swing a part by hand (or record one), then
      Groove → "From these notes" (it uses the editor's grid over a
      bar). Open a straight part and "Apply groove", with timing and
      accent amounts. Pass: the straight part takes the feel. Select an
      audio clip (a drum loop) on the timeline and "From the selected
      audio": a groove from its hits. Grooves are kept in the project.
- [ ] **3.1 MIDI learn.** Notes → MIDI learn…, pick a track and a
      setting (volume, pan, a send, an effect's setting or a plugin's
      parameter), press Learn and turn a knob. Pass: the knob moves the
      setting across its whole range; Remove unties it.

## Decisions to make

- [ ] **3.1 Where MIDI learn ties are kept.** They are in the project,
      so they travel with it; a second person's controllers would need
      ties of their own. The other way is to keep them per computer.
- [ ] **3.1 Controllers and expression when recording.** Knobs and
      pedals played while recording are not yet written into controller
      lanes (notes are). Say if that should come next.
- [ ] **3.1 Controller lanes on one channel.** Lanes play on MIDI
      channel 1; notes keep their own channels. Fine for one instrument
      per track; say if per-channel lanes are wanted.
- [ ] **3.1 Grooves as presets.** Grooves live in the project they were
      made in. A library of grooves shared between projects could come
      with the content browser.
