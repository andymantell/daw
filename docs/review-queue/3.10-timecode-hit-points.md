# 3.10 Timecode, hit points and tempo fit

Built at risk while the owner was away (plan item A12). No video is
needed for any of this: it works on any project, and the video clips
(A11) and the video pane (B7) build on it.

## On the PC

- [ ] **3.10 Timecode on the ruler.** Screenshot:
      `app-timecode-hit-points.png`. View → Timecode (or Edit →
      Timecode and hit points…, then tick "Show timecode"). Pass: a
      "Timecode" line appears above the bar numbers, labelled
      hours:minutes:seconds:frames, and the transport bar shows the
      timecode between the bar position and the seconds. Zoom in until
      single frames are labelled, and out to whole minutes: the labels
      thin out and never overlap.
- [ ] **3.10 Frame rates and drop-frame.** In the window, try each frame
      rate. Pass: at 29.97 and 30 drop-frame the labels use a semicolon
      before the frames (01:00:00;00), and a playhead just past the start of a
      minute reads ;02, not ;00, except on every tenth minute. If you
      have a video editor or a clip with burnt-in timecode, compare a few
      positions an hour in: they should match to the frame.
- [ ] **3.10 Start timecode and offset.** Set the start timecode to
      01:00:00:00 and press "At the playhead" with the playhead at bar 3.
      Pass: the ruler reads 01:00:00:00 at bar 3 and counts back into the
      previous hour before it. Hit points move with the start, as they
      belong to the picture.
- [ ] **3.10 Hit points.** Put the playhead somewhere and press H (Edit →
      Add a hit point). Right-click the ruler or the timecode line →
      "Add hit point here". Pass: a diamond with its name appears on the
      timecode line, with a dashed line down through the tracks, on the
      start of a picture frame. Drag a diamond: it moves a frame at a
      time. Click it: the playhead jumps to it. In the window rename one,
      type a new timecode, step it a frame with ◀ ▶, and delete one.
- [ ] **3.10 Tempo fit.** Screenshot: `app-tempo-fit.png`. Make three hit
      points, set "lands on" to a bar for two and a beat for one, and
      press Find tempos. Pass: up to six tempos, best first, each with
      how far off each hit point is in milliseconds and frames. Press
      "Use as the song's tempo" on one: the tempo changes and the bar
      lines move to meet the hit points, which stay put. Then choose
      "A tempo change from bar" with a bar after the first hit point,
      find again and use one: a tempo change is added on that bar and the
      bars before it don't move.
- [ ] **3.10 Saved, shared and undone.** Save, reopen, and open the
      project on the second computer. Pass: timecode settings and hit
      points are all there; Snapshots → changes lists hit points added,
      moved, renamed and removed; Edit → Undo takes back each change.

## Decisions to accept

- [ ] **Default frame rate 25 fps.** New projects start at 25 (the
      European video rate). Say if 24 (film) would suit you better.
- [ ] **Hit points are placed in time, not in bars.** They stay with the
      picture when the tempo changes, so the tempo fit moves the music to
      meet them. They sit on the start of a picture frame, and move with
      the picture when its start timecode's place or the frame rate
      changes.
- [ ] **What the fit tries.** Tempos a hundredth of a BPM apart across the
      range you give (or whole BPM only), then the best few to a
      thousandth, which is as fine as the project keeps tempos. Tempos
      whose worst miss is within the same millisecond count as equal, and
      the one nearest the tempo already there comes first. A fit with a
      tempo change puts a jump (not a glide) on beat 1 of the bar.
- [ ] **The "lands on" choices aren't saved.** They are for one fit and
      reset when the app restarts.
- [ ] **No 59.94 fps.** The roadmap's list (23.976, 24, 25, 29.97, 30, 50,
      60) is what is offered; 59.94 can be added if you need it.

Known limits: the drop-frame rule (labels ;00 and ;01 skipped each minute
except every tenth) comes from the research notes, not the standard's
own text, which isn't freely available; the tests check it against the
known values (one hour of 29.97 drop-frame is 107,892 frames). The
timeline's snapping doesn't snap to hit points or frames yet, and the fit
doesn't try glides. Hit points in the video pane came with B7: see
`3.10-scoring-to-picture.md`.
