# 3.3 Step sequencer and patterns

Built at risk while the owner was away (plan item B4). An instrument
track can now hold pattern clips: a clip that plays a drum pattern made
of lanes of steps, edited in the new pattern editor (View → Pattern
editor, key P). Several clips can play one pattern, so an edit changes
all of them. Notes anywhere can now have a chance of playing, rolled
afresh each time round, and notes can share one roll or have one of them
picked at random. Screenshots (drawn by the test harness, whose fonts
lack a few icons): `/mnt/project-files/screenshots/app-pattern-editor.png`.

Until the drum machine (plan item B3) is in, patterns play through the
track's instrument as ordinary notes, so with the simple synth a kick is
a low tone. The pattern's lanes use the standard drum note numbers
(kick 36, snare 38, closed hi-hat 42, open hi-hat 46), which the drum
machine's pads will listen for.

## On the PC

- [ ] **3.3 Adding a pattern.** Right-click an instrument track's lane
      on the timeline and choose "Add drum pattern here" (or Edit → Add
      drum pattern, which puts one at the playhead). Pass: a bar-long
      clip appears with a small grid mark before its name, the pattern
      editor opens below the timeline with Kick, Snare, Closed hi-hat
      and Open hi-hat lanes and a plain rock beat, and pressing play
      plays it.
- [ ] **3.3 Editing steps.** Click steps to turn them on and off;
      right-click a step to pick it without changing it, then set its
      velocity, pitch, chance, repeats, offset and gate below the grid.
      Pass: each change is heard on the next time round; a step with
      repeats plays a quick roll; a gate over 100% holds into the next
      step; a 50% step plays about half the time, differently each time
      round the loop.
- [ ] **3.3 Lanes.** Click a lane's name to pick it; set its steps,
      step length (1/4 to 1/32, and triplets) and direction (forward,
      backward, back and forth, random). Pass: a 1/16 triplet hi-hat
      lane of 12 steps lines up with a 16-step 1/16 kick lane in the
      grid and in time; a 5-step lane drifts against a 16-step one; the
      M button mutes a lane.
- [ ] **3.3 Euclidean fill and swing.** Set "hits" and "turned" for a
      lane and press Fill. Pass: the hits are spread evenly (5 hits over
      16 steps, say); Swing moves every second step later, up to half a
      step at 100%; velocity "±" makes repeated hits vary.
- [ ] **3.3 Linked clips.** Right-click a pattern clip → "Duplicate
      (linked)", then change a step. Pass: both clips change. Then
      "Make unique" on one and change a step again: only that one
      changes. "Convert to plain notes" turns a clip into an ordinary
      note clip with the same notes, editable in the note editor.
- [ ] **3.3 Chance on any notes.** In the note editor, select some
      notes and set Chance; select a chord and press "Play one at
      random"; select a fill and press "Play together" with chance
      at 50%. Pass: the chord plays one of its notes each time round;
      the fill plays whole or not at all.
- [ ] **3.3 Saving and changes.** Save, close and reopen. Pass: the
      patterns, their clips and links come back; Changes… lists
      "Patterns: Added/Changed …".

## Decisions to accept

- [ ] **Chance is rolled as the song plays, by the engine,** each time
      the playhead jumps (pressing play, a seek, going round the loop).
      Without a loop, a long song gets one roll per note, so each
      repeat of a pattern clip rolls separately. A frozen or rendered
      track keeps whichever rolls happened in its render.
- [ ] **Random direction and velocity variance are fixed per pattern,**
      worked out from the pattern's id: they differ from step to step
      and bar to bar, but are the same each time round the loop, so the
      timeline and the note editor show what plays. Only chance changes
      each time round.
- [ ] **Not built yet:** note-repeat while holding a key or pad (it needs
      the drum machine's pads, B3), and recording into a pattern from
      pads. Editing a single step from outside the app waits on the
      typed command layer (roadmap 2.11); adding a pattern, making one
      unique and converting to notes are commands now.
