# 3.4 Eighty (classic synths, S6)

The eight-voice two-channel touch polysynth from the classic synths
plan (`docs/plans/classic-synths.md`), for big pads, brass and
expressive leads. Every voice has two complete channels: a saw and a
square (with a pulse width its own slow sweep can move) and noise
through a resonant high-pass and a resonant low-pass (12 or 24 dB),
a sine added after the filters, a filter envelope that goes from an
initial level up to an attack level and back down while the key is
held, and a loudness envelope; the two are mixed. How hard a key is
struck and how hard it is held down afterwards set the level, the
brightness and the vibrato: polyphonic aftertouch from a keyboard, or
a note's pressure line in the editor. A sub-oscillator gives vibrato,
a filter wobble and tremolo; a ring modulator with its own envelope
works on the sum of the voices; the bucket-brigade chorus widens it;
the pitch wheel plays the ribbon. It is "Eighty" in the instrument
list, `eighty` to assistants, `add-eighty-track` in Ctrl+K.

Every setting is in the shared command table (`instrument.ch1_lp`,
`instrument.after_cutoff` and the rest, to `set_parameter`,
`set_parameters` and the automation lanes), the sounds made in code
are listed by `get_instrument` with the settings that make them, and
the end-to-end test `the_eighty_is_played_through_the_assistant` adds
the track, voices the big pad, plays a chord twice and presses on the
second one through the notes' pressure lines, which lifts and brightens
it.

## Listening

The renders are in the project files under `synths/listening/`
(`cargo run -p daw-instruments --release --example eighty_listen -- <dir>`
makes them again). Each is stereo and normalised to the same peak.
They are the seven sounds made in code and three demonstrations.

- [ ] **The pads.** `eighty-big-pad.wav` (a saw channel and a
      square channel with the width sweeping, slow envelopes, both
      chorus sweeps) and `eighty-strings.wav` (two saw channels nine
      cents apart, vibrato, chorus). Pass: wide and slow-moving, the
      square's sweep audible as a slow shimmer inside the pad, the
      chord changes smooth; say whether the big pad's filters open
      enough or want more brilliance.
- [ ] **Brass.** `eighty-brass.wav`: stabs then a held chord, two saw
      channels nine cents apart, the filter envelope from half an
      octave under the cutoff up two and a half and back. Pass: the
      "blat" of the filter envelope on each stab, and the held chord
      settling to a warm sustain rather than a buzz.
- [ ] **The lead and the bass.** `eighty-expressive-lead.wav` (one
      voice, glide, saw plus narrow pulse on one channel and saw plus
      sine on the other) and `eighty-pressure-bass.wav` (saw and square
      with a sine an octave under). In both, the key pressure is pushed
      in and let go over the line, as a player's hand would. Pass: the
      pressure opens the sound up and brings in vibrato on the lead
      without a step or a click; the bass stays solid underneath.
- [ ] **Keys and bells.** `eighty-soft-keys.wav` (a sine channel and a
      narrow pulse channel with a fast filter envelope, velocity to
      brightness) and `eighty-ring-bells.wav` (the ring modulator at
      80% with its envelope sweeping the speed down over each note).
      Pass: the keys get brighter as the velocity rises up the run;
      the bells are bell-like rather than a buzz.
- [ ] **Aftertouch.** `eighty-aftertouch.wav`: the big pad on one held
      chord, pressed in slowly from two seconds, held, and let go.
      Pass: the filters open smoothly and the level lifts with them;
      nothing steps.
- [ ] **Ring modulator.** `eighty-ring-modulator.wav`: the brass with
      the ring modulator's envelope sweeping its speed three octaves
      down over each note, its depth rising from none to full over the
      line. Pass: from plain brass through a metallic edge to clangour.
- [ ] **Chorus modes.** `eighty-chorus-modes.wav`: the strings with the
      chorus off for a bar, then I, then II, then both. Pass: the
      sides open up when it comes in, I slower than II, both the
      widest; no obvious pitch wobble.
- [ ] **In the app.** Add an Eighty track (Ctrl+K, "Add Eighty
      track"), choose **Big pad** under **Sound**, play a chord. Pass:
      it sounds like the render; draw a note's pressure line in the
      note editor (the **Note pressure** lane) and the note opens up on
      playback; with a keyboard that sends aftertouch, pressing a held
      key does the same; the controls are in six groups and each is
      reachable by an assistant.

## Decisions

- [ ] **Filters at the sample rate.** The channel filters run at the
      sample rate, not twice it as the toolkit's other filters do,
      because a voice has four of them (eight at 24 dB): eight voices
      with everything on cost about 7% of one core on the cloud box;
      oversampled they would cost about four times that. Two-pole
      filters with mild saturation alias little. Keep, or give the
      synth a quality switch that oversamples?
- [ ] **The envelope opens the high-pass too.** As on the original,
      the filter envelope, the brilliance, the touch and the keyboard
      tracking move both the high-pass and the low-pass of a channel
      together. Keep, or let the high-pass sit still?
- [ ] **Default level.** The level defaults to −6 dB after the voices
      are scaled so a four-note chord reaches the amplifier's soft
      limit; the renders peak around −10 dBFS before normalising. Right,
      or hotter?
- [ ] **Aftertouch defaults.** A new Eighty has aftertouch to level at
      40%, to brightness one octave and to vibrato 0.3 of a semitone,
      so pressure does something out of the box. Keep these on by
      default?

## Measured

Eight voices at 48 kHz in a release build on the cloud box, both
channels with saw, square, noise and sine, the high-pass on, the ring
modulator and both chorus sweeps: about 7% of one core
(`eight_voices_fit_the_budget`, release builds only; the limit is 25%).
The golden render test `a_fixed_patch_renders_to_its_known_spectrum`
holds the brass sound at C3's harmonics to within 1.5 dB.

## Not built

- The ribbon is the pitch wheel with a range setting; there is no
  separate ribbon control that bends from where it is first touched.
- The original's four memory presets and the panel's bank of
  pre-wired sounds are the sounds made in code here; there is no
  separate memory.
- The sub-oscillator's external input and the keyboard's split
  (brilliance per half) are not here.
