# 3.4 June (classic synths, S4)

The six-voice polysynth with a chorus from the classic synths plan
(`docs/plans/classic-synths.md`), for pads, chords, house stabs and
bass: one digitally controlled oscillator a voice giving a saw and a
pulse at once (the width moved by hand, the LFO or the envelope), a
square sub an octave down and noise; a four-position high-pass whose
first position lifts the bass; the 24 dB cascade low-pass that sings
on its own, moved by the envelope either way up, the LFO, the keys,
velocity and the bender; one ADSR envelope shared by filter and
amplifier, which can follow the gate instead; a triangle LFO with a
delay before it fades in; and the stereo bucket-brigade chorus, I, II
or both. Six voices by default, up to sixteen; poly, unison (every
voice on one note, detuned) or mono; portamento per voice. It is
"June" in the instrument list, `june` to assistants, `add-june-track`
in Ctrl+K.

Every setting is in the shared command table (`instrument.<key>` to
`set_parameter`, `set_parameters` and the automation lanes), the
sounds made in code are listed by `get_instrument` with the settings
that make them (an assistant gives them to `set_instrument`), and the
end-to-end test `the_june_is_played_through_the_assistant` adds the
track, voices the strings pad, plays a chord and renders it.

## Listening

The renders are in the project files under `synths/listening/`
(`cargo run -p daw-instruments --release --example june_listen -- <dir>`
makes them again). Each is stereo, normalised to the same peak. They
are the eight sounds made in code, each playing chords or a line, and
five demonstrations.

- [ ] **The pads.** `june-strings-pad.wav` (saw through the 250 Hz
      high-pass, chorus I, a slow attack) and `june-warm-pad.wav`
      (pulse with the width swept by a slow LFO, both choruses). Pass:
      the strings shimmer and the chorus moves without wobbling the
      pitch audibly; the warm pad breathes. Say whether chorus I is
      too slow or too fast against what you remember of the real thing.
- [ ] **The stab.** `june-house-stab.wav`: saw and pulse with a sub,
      the envelope snapping the filter open, chorus II. Pass: a house
      stab you could put under the deep house track; say if the
      resonance (25%) should be higher.
- [ ] **The basses.** `june-chorus-bass.wav` (saw and sub at 16' in
      mono with chorus I) and `june-round-bass.wav` (a square and the
      sub at 16', the bass-lift position of the high-pass, a little
      drive, no chorus). Pass: clean and round rather than farty; say
      which is nearer the clean round bass the song-3 wish list asked
      for.
- [ ] **Brass, organ, lead.** `june-brass.wav` (saw, the filter
      opening slowly, velocity to the filter), `june-organ.wav` (pulse
      and sub with the amplifier following the gate) and
      `june-unison-lead.wav` (six voices stacked, 12 cents apart, with
      portamento and the wheel's vibrato). Pass: each is what it says;
      the unison is thick but stays in tune.
- [ ] **The chorus modes.** `june-chorus-off-one-two-both.wav`: one
      chord four times, with the chorus off, I, II and both. Pass:
      off is mono and dry, I is slow and wide, II faster, both at once
      the thickest; a little hiss with the chorus on is meant (the
      bucket-brigade's), say if it is too much.
- [ ] **The high-pass switch.** `june-highpass-steps.wav`: a held
      chord with the switch stepping through its positions every three
      seconds: bass lift, flat, cut at 250 Hz, cut at 600 Hz. Pass:
      the first step has more bass than the second, the last two have
      less and less.
- [ ] **The filter singing.** `june-self-oscillation.wav`: no
      oscillators, resonance full, the keys playing the filter with full
      tracking through chorus I. Pass: a sine-like whistle in tune with
      the lead line.
- [ ] **The LFO's delay.** `june-lfo-delay.wav`: a held chord with the
      vibrato and the pulse-width sweep arriving a second and a half
      in. Pass: the chord starts still and the movement fades in
      rather than switching on.
- [ ] **The envelope inverted.** `june-envelope-inverted.wav`: the stab
      with the envelope opening the filter for the first bar, then
      closing it from above for the second. Pass: the second bar's
      stabs start bright and darken.
- [ ] **In the app.** Add a June track (Ctrl+K, "Add June track"),
      choose **Strings pad** under **Sound**, play a chord. Pass: it
      sounds like the render; the mixer strip says "June"; the
      controls are in groups and each is reachable by an assistant
      (`instrument.chorus` and the rest).

## Decisions

- [x] **Velocity.** The classic has none; here velocity is on by
      default (30% to the level, none to the filter), so a played clip
      has some dynamics. Keep it on, or default to none so the synth
      plays flat like the original?
- [x] **Voices.** Six by default, as the classic, up to sixteen. With
      six, a sustained pad's release tails are cut short by the next
      chord, as the original's were. Keep six, or default higher?
- [x] **The LFO's delay.** The fade-in takes a quarter of the delay
      time once the delay has passed. Keep, or make it a fixed half
      second?

## Measured

Six voices at 48 kHz with the chorus on, noise in the mix, in a
release build on the cloud box: about 6% of one core
(`six_voices_fit_the_budget`, release builds only), so about 1% a
voice; sixteen voices would be about 16%, over the plan's "few
percent", which a later pass running the voices in lockstep (SIMD)
would bring down. The golden render test
`the_golden_stab_renders_the_same_spectrum` holds a two-note stab's
harmonics to within 1 dB.

## Not built

- No pitch drift: the oscillators are digitally controlled, so
  **Vintage drift** moves each voice's pulse width, level and filter
  cutoff a little instead.
- The chorus is one for the whole synth, after the voices, as the
  circuit's is; there is no per-voice stereo spread.
- The LFO runs from the first key down; there is no manual trigger
  button.
