# 3.4 Lucky Seven (classic synths, S8)

The six-operator FM synth from the classic synths plan
(`docs/plans/classic-synths.md`), for electric pianos, bells, FM bass,
brass and plucks. Six sine operators, each with a frequency as a ratio
of the note (coarse, fine, detune) or fixed in Hz, an output level on
the 0 to 99 scale, a rate/level envelope with four rates and four
levels, level scaling either side of a break point with linear or
exponential curves, rate scaling up the keyboard, velocity and
LFO-to-level sensitivities. Thirty-two algorithms say which operator
modulates which and which are heard, with feedback round one of them.
An LFO with six shapes to pitch and to level, delayed and keyed; a
pitch envelope; a transpose; sixteen voices, or one with glide and
legato. It is "Lucky Seven" in the instrument list, `lucky-seven` to
assistants, `add-lucky-seven-track` in Ctrl+K.

It reads the public bank format for your own sounds: **Load a bank…**
in its window (or `load-synth-bank` in Ctrl+K) takes a system-exclusive
file of thirty-two packed sounds or one sound, sets the first and
lists the rest under **From the bank**. No sounds from anyone else come
with the app. The scales are the format's own (levels, rates and depths
0 to 99, sensitivities 0 to 7), so a sound written down for the
original reads straight in.

Every setting is in the shared command table (`instrument.algorithm`,
`instrument.op1_level`, `instrument.op2_rate1` and the rest, 151 in
all, to `set_parameter`, `set_parameters` and the automation lanes),
the sounds made in code are listed by `get_instrument` with the
settings that make them, a loaded bank's sounds are listed there too,
`file_command` with `load-synth-bank` loads a bank, and the end-to-end
test `the_lucky_seven_is_played_through_the_assistant` adds the track,
voices the tine piano, plays a note soft and hard (the hard one is
brighter), loads a bank from a file and sets one of its sounds.

## Listening

The renders are in the project files under `synths/listening/`
(`cargo run -p daw-instruments --release --example lucky_seven_listen -- <dir>`
makes them again). Each is normalised to the same peak. They are the
seven sounds made in code and five demonstrations.

- [ ] **Tine piano.** `lucky-seven-tine-piano.wav`: four chords.
      Pass: a bell-like tine on top of a warm body that fades first,
      the tine stronger on the louder chords; no buzz on the low notes.
      `lucky-seven-velocity.wav` is the same note eight times from soft
      to hard: the tine should come in as it gets harder, as the
      original's does.
- [ ] **Bells.** `lucky-seven-tubular-bells.wav`: a bell line with
      inharmonic partials (ratios 1 against 3.5 and 1.73). Pass: bell
      rather than organ; the hum under it lasts longest.
- [ ] **Solid bass.** `lucky-seven-solid-bass.wav`: a bass line, one
      voice, with a quick glide. Pass: a plucky bite on each note that
      settles to a round tone; the low notes stay clean.
- [ ] **Brass section.** `lucky-seven-brass-section.wav`: stabs then
      a held chord. Pass: the swell of each stab from a brass player's
      breath (the modulators come in slightly after the carrier), a
      little vibrato arriving on the held chord.
- [ ] **Pluck.** `lucky-seven-pluck.wav`: a line up the keyboard.
      Pass: bright at the start of each note and dying to a plain tone,
      quicker on the higher notes (rate scaling).
- [ ] **Glass pad and drawbar organ.** `lucky-seven-glass-pad.wav`
      (slow chords, the modulators detuned so it moves) and
      `lucky-seven-drawbar-organ.wav` (six sines held, a slight
      tremolo). Pass: the pad glassy and slow-moving without wobbling
      out of tune; the organ steady, with the drawbars audible as a
      stack of octaves and fifths.
- [ ] **Feedback.** `lucky-seven-feedback-steps.wav`: one operator
      with its feedback stepped from 0 to 7, a second each. Pass: from
      a sine to a saw-like tone by 7, with no step turning to noise.
- [ ] **A modulator's level.** `lucky-seven-modulator-sweep.wav`: a
      modulator swept from 0 to 99 over a held note. Pass: nothing
      happens for the first half (the level scale is quiet below 50),
      then it brightens to a buzz at the top; whether that matches the
      feel of the original's level knob is the main thing to say.
- [ ] **Pitch envelope and LFO shapes.** `lucky-seven-pitch-envelope.wav`
      (a fifth down into each note) and `lucky-seven-lfo-shapes.wav`
      (triangle, saw down, saw up, square, sine, sample-and-hold, two
      seconds each, to pitch and level). Pass: the shapes are what they
      say.
- [ ] **In the app.** Add a Lucky Seven track (Ctrl+K, "Add Lucky
      Seven track"), choose **Tine piano** under **Sound**, play. Pass:
      it sounds like the render; the operator groups are closed to
      start with and open to the format's own numbers; **Load a bank…**
      reads one of your own `.syx` files and **From the bank** lists
      its sounds; each control is reachable by an assistant.

## Decisions

- [x] **The level scale and the modulation depth.** A modulator at
      level 99 deviates the carrier's phase by four pi; each level step
      is three quarters of a decibel, steeper under 20. These are from
      published descriptions, not measurements, so sounds from a bank
      may come out a little brighter or duller than on the original.
      If a bank you know sounds wrong in one direction, say so and the
      two constants (`INDEX`, `level_to_amp`) move together.
- [x] **Envelope rates.** A rate of 99 crosses the range in 4 ms, 50
      in half a second, 0 in over a minute, with rate scaling adding
      up to the scaling setting per octave above A1. Right against a
      bank you know?
- [x] **Feedback.** Feedback 7 is set so a lone operator turns
      saw-like and not into noise (1.5 radians times the average of
      its last two outputs). The original is reputed to get noisy at 7
      on some sounds; keep it clean, or let it go further?
- [x] **Release tails.** A sound whose carriers' fourth level is above
      zero would ring forever on the original (until its voice is
      taken). Here the voice fades over 20 ms once every carrier has
      reached its fourth level. Keep, or hold the voice until it is
      stolen?
- [x] **The playing settings aren't in a bank.** Voices, glide, bend
      range, the wheel depth and the level come back to their defaults
      when a bank sound is chosen, since the format has no place for
      them. Right, or keep what was set?

## Measured

Sixteen voices at 48 kHz in a release build on the cloud box, all six
operators running, the brass sound: about 13 to 15% of one core
(`sixteen_voices_fit_the_budget`, release builds only; the limit is
25%). The envelopes run in amplitude so a sample costs an add or a
multiply; the sine is a table. Halving that would need the operators
of a voice run in lockstep (SIMD), the same next step as the other
synths. The golden render test `the_golden_tine_renders_the_same_spectrum`
holds the tine piano at C4 to 1.5 dB at five partials.

## Not built

- Writing banks out, and the function and performance parameters of
  the format beyond the sound (the wheel's depth is a plain setting
  here; aftertouch and the wheel both bring in the LFO's pitch depth).
- The original's two voice memories and cartridge are the bank file
  and the sounds made in code.
- Microtuning and the later models' extras.
