# 3.4 Oh-One (classic synths, S3)

The one-oscillator bass and lead monosynth of the early 1980s, built on
the vintage toolkit (`crates/instruments/src/oh_one.rs`, plan
`docs/plans/classic-synths.md`). "Oh-One" in the instrument list,
`oh-one` to assistants, `add-oh-one-track` in Ctrl+K.

What it has, as the plan asks: one oscillator giving a saw and a pulse
at once (the pulse's width moved by hand, by the LFO or by the
envelope), a sub-oscillator an octave down, two down, or two down as a
narrow pulse, noise, a four-position range switch (16' to 2'), a source
mixer that warms the filter's input when pushed, the 24 dB
transconductance cascade filter (self-oscillating at full resonance)
swept by the envelope, the LFO, the keyboard and the bender, one ADSR
with analogue curves, an amplifier that follows the envelope or just the
gate, an LFO with triangle, square, random and noise shapes, portamento
that is off, on, or only between overlapping notes, lowest, highest or
last-note priority, legato, and the toolkit's drift under **Vintage
drift**. Every setting is in the shared settings table, so
`list_parameters`, `set_parameter`, `set_parameters`, `get_instrument`
and automation lanes reach them all.

## Listening

The renders are in the project files under `synths/listening/`
(`cargo run -p daw-instruments --release --example oh_one_listen -- <dir>`
makes them again). Each is normalised to the same peak.

- [ ] **House bass.** `oh-one-house-bass.wav`: saw, a little pulse with
      slow width movement, the sub two octaves down, cutoff at 300 Hz
      with resonance at 55% and a 2½-octave envelope sweep, the gate
      amplifier, slides only between overlapping notes. Pass: it has the
      weight and the "bounce" of a classic house bass, with the octave
      jumps sliding and the rest not.
- [ ] **Acid squelch.** `oh-one-acid-squelch.wav`: saw through the
      filter at 85% resonance with a 3½-octave sweep, drive at 60%,
      legato with slides, the second half accented harder. Pass: it
      squelches and the resonance sings without going thin; compare
      with the diode-ladder acid line from the toolkit's renders.
- [ ] **Pulse-width lead.** `oh-one-pwm-lead.wav`: pulse alone with the
      LFO moving its width, an octave up, 60% drift, portamento on.
      Pass: it shimmers on its own and sounds like one old oscillator,
      not a chorus.
- [ ] **Sub bass.** `oh-one-sub-bass.wav`: saw with the narrow-pulse sub
      two octaves down, the filter nearly closed. Pass: deep, round and
      clean, no "farty" wah.
- [ ] **The filter singing.** `oh-one-self-oscillation.wav`: no
      oscillator, resonance full, keyboard tracking full, so the filter
      plays the melody with glide. Pass: a clean whistle in tune with
      the notes.
- [ ] **Random LFO on the filter.** `oh-one-random-filter.wav`: the
      random shape stepping the cutoff at 8 Hz over two held notes.
      Pass: a burbling sample-and-hold, in time.
- [ ] **In the app.** Ctrl+K, "Add Oh-One track", play a bass. Pass: the
      settings window shows the six groups, every control is reachable
      by an assistant, and an assistant can voice a bass by name (the
      end-to-end test `the_oh_one_is_voiced_played_and_read_through_the_assistant`
      does this: adds the synth, sets a house bass from the settings,
      plays a bar with a slide, renders it and reads the settings back).

## Measured

- CPU, release build on the cloud test machine (slower than a desktop):
  one voice at 2× oversampling, 215 ns a sample, about 1% of one core at
  48 kHz. The plan's budget is a few percent; the test
  `one_voice_fits_the_budget` holds it under 5%.
- Golden render: a fixed bass patch's levels at half, one, two, three
  and four times its fundamental are checked within half a decibel
  (`the_golden_bass_renders_the_same_spectrum`), so a change to the
  toolkit can't quietly change the sound.
- Pitch follows the note and the range switch within 1%; the sub adds
  the octave below; the filter closes by 30 dB at the eighth harmonic
  and sings within 5% of its cutoff with no source; pulse width follows
  the LFO and the envelope; gate mode holds full level until the key
  lifts; portamento glides only when asked; priority and legato follow
  their settings (all unit tests).

## Decisions to accept

- [ ] **Saw and pulse together partly cancel at the fundamental.** Both
      come from one oscillator core, and the pulse's fundamental is
      opposite in sign to the saw's, so mixing them gives the hollow
      "saw plus square" tone rather than a louder saw. That is how a
      single-core oscillator mixes, but the polarity of the real
      circuit's pulse is not in the sources read. Keep, or flip the
      pulse so the two add?
- [ ] **Default level.** At its default −6 dB a bass peaks around
      −14 dBFS, on the quiet side next to the other instruments. Raise
      the default, or leave the amplifier's headroom as it is?
- [ ] **Velocity.** The original had no velocity; here it sets the
      level at 50% by default so note clips with dynamics do something.
      Keep, or default it to off for the authentic flat response?
