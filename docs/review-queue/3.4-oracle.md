# 3.4 Oracle (classic synths, S5)

The two-oscillator analogue polysynth with two filter boards from the
classic synths plan (`docs/plans/classic-synths.md`), for brass, pads,
leads and bass: sixteen voices, each with two oscillators whose saw,
triangle and pulse can all sound at once, pulse-width modulation,
oscillator A hard-synced to B, oscillator B free of the keyboard or
slowed to a modulator; poly-mod (the filter envelope and oscillator B
into oscillator A's pitch and width and the filter cutoff, per voice);
noise; filter board A, the 4-pole cascade, or board B, the 2-pole
state-variable filter with low, notch, high and band outputs; two ADSRs
with velocity; an LFO whose saw, triangle and square can be on at once,
from an initial amount and the wheel; unison stacking with detune and
spread; glide in every voice; and a vintage amount that drifts the
oscillators and scatters each voice's filter and envelope speeds. It is
"Oracle" in the instrument list, `oracle` to assistants,
`add-oracle-track` in Ctrl+K.

Every setting is in the shared command table (`instrument.<key>` to
`set_parameter`, `set_parameters` and the automation lanes), the sounds
made in code are listed by `get_instrument` with the settings that make
them (an assistant gives them to `set_instrument`), and the end-to-end
test `the_oracle_is_played_through_the_assistant` adds the track, voices
the warm pad on the notch output with unison, plays a chord and renders
it.

## Listening

The renders are in the project files under `synths/listening/`
(`cargo run -p daw-instruments --release --example oracle_listen -- <dir>`
makes them again). Each is normalised to the same peak. They are the
eight sounds made in code, each playing chords or a line, and eight
demonstrations.

- [ ] **Brass and bass.** `oracle-poly-brass.wav` (two saws a little
      apart, the cascade opened by a quick envelope, stabs) and
      `oracle-poly-bass.wav` (saw and pulse with B an octave under, the
      cascade low, velocity into the filter). Pass: the brass has the
      bite of a sweep that sings; the bass is round with weight and no
      "farty" wah. Say if the brass envelope should open slower.
- [ ] **Pads and strings.** `oracle-warm-pad.wav` (triangle and pulse
      with the LFO on the width, the state-variable low-pass, slow
      envelopes) and `oracle-strings.wav` (saw and pulse on both
      oscillators, the notch output, slow pulse-width modulation). Pass:
      they move without a plain vibrato; the notch gives the strings
      their hollow middle.
- [ ] **Leads.** `oracle-sync-lead.wav` (A synced to B an octave down,
      the filter envelope sweeping A's pitch, one voice with glide) and
      `oracle-unison-lead.wav` (six voices on each note, 14 cents apart,
      spread across the sides). Pass: the sync sweep tears as the
      classic's did; the unison is wide and thick but stays in tune.
- [ ] **Bell and keys.** `oracle-bell.wav` (B a twelfth up into A's
      pitch, poly-mod, no filter sweep) and `oracle-hollow-keys.wav`
      (narrow and wide pulses, B an octave up). Pass: the bell is
      clangorous and inharmonic on purpose; the keys are hollow.
- [ ] **The two boards.** `oracle-board-a-cascade.wav` and
      `oracle-board-b-svf.wav`: the same chords swept by hand with
      resonance at 55%, through board A (4-pole) then board B (2-pole
      low-pass). Pass: A is darker and its resonance sharper; B is
      brighter and rounder, as the two American polys' boards were.
- [ ] **Board B's outputs.** `oracle-board-b-outputs.wav`: one chord,
      the output switched every 2.4 s: low, notch, high, band. Pass:
      four clearly different colours, the band thin and nasal.
- [ ] **Sync sweep.** `oracle-sync-sweep.wav`: one note, oscillator A
      swept from a fifth to two octaves over B and back by hand. Pass:
      the classic sync sweep, with no clicks at the resets.
- [ ] **Poly-mod into the filter.** `oracle-polymod-filter.wav`: the
      bass with oscillator B (a fifth up, silent itself) into the cutoff
      at audio rate, the amount rising from nothing to 60%. Pass: it
      goes from the plain bass to a ring-modulated growl.
- [ ] **The wheel.** `oracle-wheel-lfo.wav`: a chord with the wheel
      pushed up from 2 s to 4.5 s (the LFO to both pitches), then from 6 s
      also into the filter. Pass: a vibrato that deepens, then a wobble
      on the cutoff.
- [ ] **Vintage.** `oracle-vintage-off.wav` and `oracle-vintage-full.wav`:
      the strings chord with no drift, then with the vintage amount full.
      Pass: the full one shimmers and the voices never quite line up; say
      whether the default (40%) is about right.
- [ ] **In the app.** Add an Oracle track (Ctrl+K, "Add Oracle track"),
      choose **Poly brass** under **Sound**, play chords. Pass: it sounds
      like the render; the wheel adds vibrato; **Voices** at 1 with
      **Legato** on plays one line with glide; the controls are in groups
      and each is reachable by an assistant (`instrument.cutoff` and the
      rest).

## Decisions

- [ ] **Poly-mod pitch law.** Oscillator B into oscillator A's pitch
      goes through the exponential pitch input, as the classic's did, so
      deep amounts shift the tone and go inharmonic (the bell). A linear
      law would keep the pitch and sound more like FM synthesis. Keep
      exponential?
- [ ] **Oscillator B's low mode range.** In low-frequency mode B runs
      from 1 to 16 Hz by its frequency knob, fixed, not following the
      keys. Wide enough?
- [ ] **Default voices.** Eight, as the bigger of the two classics; the
      box holds sixteen. Eight voices cost about 7% of one core on the
      cloud box, so sixteen would be about 14%. Keep eight?
- [ ] **Velocity defaults.** Velocity to level at 50% and to the filter
      at 30%; the originals had none (one had it later). Keep?

## Measured

Eight voices at 48 kHz in a release build on the cloud box, with both
pulses on, noise, the LFO on the width and poly-mod on: about 7% of one
core (`eight_voices_cost_little`, release builds only; its limit is
25%). The golden render test `a_fixed_patch_renders_to_its_known_spectrum`
holds the poly brass at C3's harmonics to within 1.5 dB.

## Not built

- A chorus or stereo output beyond the unison spread: the classics were
  mono; a chorus belongs to S4 (the DCO poly).
- Polyphonic aftertouch and the ribbon are S6's.
- Oscillator B's envelope-shaped low-frequency "one-shot" and the
  keyboard mode switches of the later revisions.
