# 3.4 Patch Twenty (classic synths, S7)

The semi-modular dual-filter monosynth from the classic synths plan
(`docs/plans/classic-synths.md`, S7), built on the vintage toolkit:
`crates/instruments/src/patch_twenty.rs`. "Patch Twenty" in the
instrument list, `patch-twenty` to assistants, `add-patch-twenty-track`
in Ctrl+K.

What it has, as the plan asks: two oscillators (triangle, saw, pulse or
noise; saw, square, narrow pulse or a ring modulator of the two) with
32'–4' and 16'–2' ranges; a 12 dB high-pass into a 12 dB low-pass, each
the toolkit's Sallen-Key pair whose peak path clips, so both sing at
full peak and scream when driven; a delay-attack-release envelope and a
hold-ADSR; a modulation generator whose shape sweeps from a falling saw
through a triangle to a rising one (its square and a sample-and-hold of
noise come along); an external signal input through the filters and
amplifier with an envelope follower that can trigger the envelopes; and
four patch cords (source, destination, signed amount) in place of the
panel's jacks. Drive is the gain into the high-pass, whose clipping is
the mixer's overdrive. The oscillators drift ("Vintage drift"), the
envelopes have the toolkit's analogue curves and restart from where they
are, mono with low/high/last priority, retrigger or legato, portamento.

Four sounds come with it as track presets (Presets window, or
`add_preset`): "Patch Twenty screaming lead", "Patch Twenty bass",
"Patch Twenty drone" and "Guitar through Patch Twenty".

## Listening

The renders are in the project files under `synths/listening/`
(`cargo run -p daw-instruments --release --example patch_twenty_listen -- <dir>`
makes them again). Each is normalised to the same peak.

- [ ] **Screaming lead.** `patch-twenty-screaming-lead.wav`: pulse
      against a ring modulator, both filters peaking, envelope 2 pushing
      the low-pass, the mod generator moving the pulse width. Pass: it
      is aggressive and alive, the peaks sing rather than whistle
      politely, and nothing fizzes or aliases on the top notes.
- [ ] **Bass.** `patch-twenty-bass.wav`: saw an octave down with a
      square under it, the low-pass snapping shut, half drive, a short
      glide. Pass: weight and a snap at the front of each note, not a
      thin "wah" (the complaint that started the plan).
- [ ] **Drone.** `patch-twenty-drone.wav`: no key held after the first
      note; the amplifier's initial gain holds it open while the mod
      generator sweeps the low-pass and its sample-and-hold steps the
      high-pass. Pass: it moves on its own for twelve seconds without
      repeating obviously.
- [ ] **Self-oscillation.** `patch-twenty-self-oscillation.wav`: no
      oscillators, both peaks full, the keyboard driving both cutoffs
      through a patch cord, so the whistle plays a melody with glide.
      Pass: two tones (the high-pass and the low-pass an octave apart)
      that follow the notes; it is meant to be rougher and louder than
      the ladder's whistle. Say if the level or roughness is wrong.
- [ ] **Guitar through the filters.** `patch-twenty-guitar-through-the-filters.wav`
      against `patch-twenty-guitar-dry.wav`: a plucked-string stand-in
      (noise through a feedback delay, made in code) played through the
      synth with no oscillators; each pluck triggers the envelope, which
      sweeps the low-pass. Pass: the chords come out as a synth-like
      "wah" that follows the playing, and it feels like something to try
      with a real guitar.

## On the PC

- [ ] **Guitar in.** Add a Patch Twenty track (Ctrl+K, "Add Patch
      Twenty track"), add the "Guitar through Patch Twenty" preset or
      switch on **External input** in its window, pick the guitar's
      input on the track's strip in the mixer (the **In** picker shows
      on this instrument's strip, where other instrument tracks have
      none) and turn on **Monitor**. Play. Pass: the guitar comes
      through the filters only (no dry signal), the envelope opens the
      low-pass on each note, and the threshold (**Trigger threshold**)
      can be set so quiet noise doesn't trigger it. Note the latency you
      hear is the usual monitoring latency, nothing the synth adds.
- [ ] **In the app.** Play the four presets from a keyboard. Pass: they
      sound like the renders; the controls are in the instrument window
      in their groups, and an assistant reaches every one
      (`instrument.lp_cutoff`, `instrument.patch1_from`, …).

## Measured

- Both filters are the toolkit's Sallen-Key pair, measured in
  `3.4-vintage-toolkit.md` (6 dB down at the cutoff, 12 dB an octave,
  resonance leaves the bass alone, rings within 5% of the set cutoff).
  Tests here check the high-pass thins the bass by over 15 dB an octave
  and a half below its cutoff, the low-pass the top by over 15 dB, that
  the two are in series, and that each rings at its cutoff with the peak
  full.
- A fixed patch's harmonic levels are pinned by a golden test
  (`the_fixed_patch_renders_the_known_spectrum`), so a later change to
  the toolkit that alters the sound fails it rather than slipping by.
- CPU: one voice through the two oversampled filters is 2.1% of one
  core at 48 kHz on the cloud test machine (slower than a desktop) in a
  release build, within the plan's budget. The two filters each solve a
  clipped feedback loop by Newton's method at twice the rate, which is
  most of it.

## Decisions to accept

- [x] **The external input comes from the track's strip.** The guitar
      reaches the synth as the track's audio input with Monitor on (and
      any audio clip on the track plays through it too), rather than
      through a picker in the synth's window: the input picker appears
      on this instrument's mixer strip, and `set_track_input` allows it
      for this instrument where it refuses other instrument tracks.
      Alternative: a picker inside the window, which would need the
      window to know about devices.
- [x] **Patch cords instead of jacks.** Four cords, each a source, a
      destination and a signed amount (−100% to 100%, so an inverted
      envelope is a negative amount) stand in for the panel's patch
      jacks and the "reversed" outputs. Sources: mod generator triangle
      and square, both envelopes, sample-and-hold, noise, keyboard,
      velocity, the external follower. Destinations: either or both
      pitches, pulse width, either or both cutoffs, the amplifier, the
      mod generator's rate. Say if four is too few or a source or
      destination is missing (the original also offered its
      pitch-to-voltage and trigger outputs from the external input, for
      a guitar to play the oscillators; not built, see below).
- [x] **Note priority defaults to last note**, with lowest and highest
      as choices, like the other monosynths here. Say if you'd rather
      the default were lowest.
- [ ] **Which of the four sounds ship**, and under what names, with the
      rest of the "which sounds ship" decision.

## Not built

- The external input's pitch-to-voltage converter (a guitar playing
  the oscillators' pitch): would need pitch tracking on the audio
  thread; the pitch readout work (Phase 4) could feed it later.
- A clock input for the sample-and-hold (it is clocked by the mod
  generator's square) and the keyboard's own trigger output.
