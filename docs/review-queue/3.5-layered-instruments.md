# 3.5 Layered instruments

Same `daw.exe`. Built at risk while the owner was away, with the simple
synth as the only instrument (plugin and built-in instruments are being
built alongside, and become layer choices as they land). Nothing here
has been tried with a MIDI keyboard or real plugins yet. Engine
behaviour is covered by tests (`crates/engine/tests/layers.rs`,
`crates/engine/src/layers.rs`); the app's by `crates/app/src/layers.rs`.

Screenshots: `app-layered-instruments.png` (the instrument window with a
three-layer keyboard split) and `app-layer-effects.png` (each layer's
effects in the effects window).

## What was built

- **Instrument → Layered instruments** in an instrument track's
  instrument window (also **Notes → Add an instrument layer**). The
  track's instrument becomes Layer 1, still playing, and a Layer 2 is
  added with the simple synth.
- Each layer has its own **On** switch, **Level**, **Pan**, **Notes**
  range (lowest to highest), **Velocities** range (softest to loudest),
  **Transpose** in semitones, and its instrument's sound settings. All
  of them change as they move, without a gap in the sound.
- Every layer hears every note: a keyboard split is two layers with
  ranges side by side; a doubled sound is two layers on the whole
  keyboard; a velocity layer is two with velocity ranges side by side.
- **Each layer has its own effects**, before the track's own: in the
  effects window each layer is a chain with its own **Add…** list, and
  an effect's **Move** menu can put it in any layer. Layers are lined up
  in time, as a container's chains are, so a plugin with latency in one
  layer doesn't put it out of step with the others.
- **▲ ▼** reorder layers, **✖** removes one with its effects. **Notes →
  Back to one instrument** keeps Layer 1's instrument; its effects move
  to the start of the track's chain and the other layers go.
- Saved in the project, with undo, and shown in **Changes…**.

## Decisions taken while you were away

- [ ] **Ranges are of the notes as written or played.** With practice
      **Transpose** on, a split stays where it is on the keyboard rather
      than moving with the transpose. Pass: you're happy with that.
- [ ] **A note keeps the layer and pitch it started with.** Changing a
      layer's range or transpose while a note is held lets that note
      finish as it began; the change is heard from the next note.
- [ ] **Layers are added together**, at their own levels, with no
      automatic lowering: two layers on the whole keyboard are louder
      than one, as two tracks would be.
- [ ] **Turning a track's instrument into layers, or back, stops the
      notes sounding at that moment** (only when playing). Changes to a
      layer's settings never do.
- [ ] **No solo per layer** yet; switch the others off to hear one.

## On the PC

- [ ] **3.5 Keyboard split.** On an instrument track with a MIDI
      keyboard, choose **Layered instruments**, set Layer 1's notes to
      C-1 to B3 with transpose −12 and a low cutoff, and Layer 2's to C4
      to G9. Pass: playing either side of middle C sounds the right
      layer, with no stuck notes when you hold a note across a range
      change.
- [ ] **3.5 Velocity layers.** Two layers on the whole keyboard,
      velocities 1–89 and 90–127, with different waves. Pass: playing
      harder switches sound.
- [ ] **3.5 An effect in one layer.** Add a reverb (or a plugin with
      latency) to Layer 2 only. Pass: only Layer 2 is reverberant, and
      the two layers stay together in time.
- [ ] **3.5 Saving, undo and back to one.** Save, reopen, undo a
      layer change, then **Back to one instrument**. Pass: everything
      comes back as it was, and Layer 1 plays on its own with its
      effects.
- [ ] **3.5 Performance.** Four layers with an effect each, playing
      chords. Pass: Settings → Performance shows no overloads.
