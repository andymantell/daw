# 3.9 Video clips

Drop a video file on the window (or open it with `daw film.mp4`). Its
picture goes in a **Video** band above the tracks, as a filmstrip of
frames, and the **Video** pane opens beside the timeline showing the frame
at the playhead. Its sound comes in as an ordinary audio clip on a track
of its own, so speed, transpose and looping work as for any audio, and it
moves when the picture is dragged. Screenshots from the cloud (a made-up
test pattern, Linux), in `/mnt/project-files/screenshots/`:
`app-video-filmstrip.png`, `app-video-zoomed.png`,
`app-video-every-frame.png`.

The picture is decoded by Windows' own media framework, so the app
carries no codec code. On Windows this was built and tried only on CI's
build machines; it has not been run on a real PC yet.

- [ ] **3.9 A phone or camera video opens.** Drop an MP4 from a phone or
      camera, and a MOV or MKV if you have one. Pass: the band shows its
      frames within a few seconds ("Filmstrip n%" counts up meanwhile),
      the Video pane shows the picture the right way up and in the right
      colours, and a track named after the file has its sound.
- [ ] **3.9 The filmstrip follows the zoom.** Ctrl + wheel over the
      timeline. Pass: zoomed out, pictures sit side by side without
      squashing; zoomed in, more frames appear; zoomed right in, each
      frame has its own box as long as the frame lasts, and a cut in the
      film shows exactly where it happens. Scrolling stays smooth.
- [ ] **3.9 The picture keeps up with playback.** Play a minute of the
      film. Pass: the pane's picture moves smoothly and stays in step
      with its own sound (a clap or a door slam lines up by eye and ear);
      jumping with a click on the ruler, looping and pressing Home show
      the new place straight away.
- [ ] **3.9 At 75% speed.** Set Speed to 75% and play. Pass: the picture
      slows with the sound and stays in step.
- [ ] **3.9 Moving the picture moves its sound.** Drag the filmstrip
      along (it snaps to the grid; hold Alt for free). Pass: the sound
      clip moves the same amount; Ctrl+Z puts both back. Right-click the
      filmstrip: "Move to the start of the song" and "Remove the
      picture" work.
- [ ] **3.9 Saved, reopened and shared.** Save, close and reopen, and make
      a bundle and open it on another computer if you can. Pass: the
      video is copied into the project's folder and comes back in place;
      the bundle carries it.
- [ ] **3.9 A file Windows can't play.** Drop a video in a format Windows
      lacks a decoder for (some WebM or AV1 files). Pass: a clear message
      naming the file, and its sound still comes in if it can be read.
- [ ] **3.9 A long film.** Drop a film of an hour or more. Pass: the app
      stays responsive while the filmstrip is made, and memory use stays
      reasonable (the filmstrip keeps at most 2000 small pictures from the
      first pass, about 30 MB, plus frames asked for when zoomed in).

## Decisions to accept

- [ ] **The system decodes video.** Each system's own media framework
      reads the picture, as the research proposed: no codec code or
      licences in the app, but which files open depends on the computer.
      On Linux the system's framework must be installed (it nearly always
      is). macOS shows "this computer can't play video yet" until a Mac
      version is written.
- [ ] **A video band above the tracks.** Pictures sit in one band under
      the ruler rather than on a track, and the last video added shows on
      top where two overlap. Its sound is a normal track you can mute,
      solo, move or delete.
- [ ] **Saving copies the whole video into the project's folder**, as for
      audio, so it travels in bundles and shared folders. A large film
      makes the project folder large, and the first save takes as long as
      copying the file.
