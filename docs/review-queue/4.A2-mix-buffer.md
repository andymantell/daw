# A2 Low-latency monitoring with a big mix buffer

Same `daw.exe`. **Settings → Audio device…** has a new **Mix buffer**
choice under **Buffer size**; **Big mix buffer on or off** in the command
search (Ctrl+K) turns it on at the last size used (2048 at first) or off.
It is off unless chosen, and it is saved with each device's settings.

With it on, armed and monitored tracks, the buses they play through, and
anything a live effect listens to as a sidechain play at the buffer size
in the device's own callback. Everything else plays ahead on a thread of
its own in blocks of the mix buffer. Both play the same place on the
timeline through the same delay compensation, so they stay in time.
Play, stop and other changes reach the ears a block or two later.

The engine proves it in the cloud (`crates/engine/tests/ahead.rs`): the
split mix comes out the same as the unsplit one, sample for sample
(within float rounding), with buses, sends, latency, a sidechain, a loop,
the metronome and practice speed; monitored input comes out in the very
callback it went in; arming and disarming move a track's effects across
and back without losing them; and a late mix thread pauses the backing
while the guitar keeps playing. These checks are about how it feels on
the PC, with real plugins and the UMC1820.

Found on the way and fixed: at practice speed (slowed or transposed), a
bus went silent and a monitored input was not heard, because the
stretcher replaced what was routed into the track instead of adding to
it.

## On the PC

- [ ] **A2 Amp at a small buffer.** Load a heavy song (several plugin
      instruments and effects) that drops out at 64 or 128 samples. Set
      **Buffer size** to 64 (or the smallest that works) and **Mix
      buffer** to 2048. Arm a guitar track with the amp on it and monitor.
      Pass: playing through the amp feels immediate, the song plays with
      no dropouts, and the Audio device window says how much of its time
      the mix is using.
- [ ] **A2 In time.** Record a take along with the song, with the mix
      buffer on. Pass: the take lands where it was played, as with it off.
- [ ] **A2 Arm and disarm while playing.** Arm and disarm the guitar track
      while the song plays. Pass: at most a short gap in that track's
      sound, never a burst without its effects, and the amp still works
      after doing it a few times. Tell me how the gap feels.
- [ ] **A2 Plugin editors.** With the mix buffer on, open a plugin's
      window on an armed track and on another one, and turn their knobs.
      Pass: both respond (the armed one now runs in the device callback,
      the other on the mix thread).
- [ ] **A2 Response.** Press play and stop a few times with the mix buffer
      at 4096. Pass: the delay (about a tenth of a second at 48 kHz) is
      acceptable; otherwise say which size feels right.
- [ ] **A2 Default.** Decide whether the mix buffer should be on by
      default, and at which size.

## Known limits

- Headphone mixes, the meters and the playhead show the mix as heard; the
  meters of the tracks playing ahead move a block or two early.
- If the mix thread falls behind (an overloaded computer), the backing
  pauses and picks up where it stopped, rather than skipping; the live
  tracks play on. The Audio device window counts the samples it paused
  for.
- The mix thread asks Windows for a time-critical priority. Other systems
  run it at normal priority for now.
- Stop, seek and other transport commands take effect after what the mix
  thread already made has played, up to a mix buffer later. A take
  (at any practice speed) runs on until the stop is heard.
