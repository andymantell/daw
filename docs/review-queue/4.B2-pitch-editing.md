# 4.B2 Pitch editing, note by note

Built-in pitch editing for a single voice or a single-note guitar or
bass line. Select an audio clip, open **View → Pitch editor**, and press
**Find notes**. The notes come up as blobs on a piano-roll grid, with
the pitch line as sung drawn faintly and the line as it now plays drawn
over each blob.

- **Click** a note to choose it (Shift adds more). **Drag** it up or
  down to move its pitch by semitones (hold **Alt** for cents), and
  sideways to move it in time. **Drag its right end** to make it longer
  or shorter. The audio either side is stretched or squeezed to make
  room, never more than 3×. Where it was sung stays outlined.
- The bar above sets, for the chosen notes (or all of them): **Pitch**
  (semitones, with cents as the fraction), **Drift kept** (100 % as
  sung, 0 % holds the note flat on its pitch), **Vibrato** (0 % takes it
  out, up to 300 % deepens it) and **Formants** (down sounds bigger, up
  smaller).
- Buttons, all in the shared command table: **Snap to notes**, **Up a
  semitone**, **Down a semitone**, **Flatten drift**, **As sung** and
  **Find notes again**. Finding again keeps each new note's pitch edits
  from the old note it mostly covers.

Nothing is rendered to a file. The edits are saved with the clip and
played live through the stretcher, as clip transpose is. Edits undo
like any other, and a whole drag on the grid is one step. Export, freeze and render
in place play the edits too.

How notes are found: the tuner's pitch detector reads the pitch every
10 ms. A new note starts where the pitch moves to another note and
stays there, where the voice stops, or where the level dips and comes
back (the same note sung twice). The assistant's note-by-note readout
(`analyse` with `notes: true`) now uses the same detector.

Assistants: `edit_pitch_notes` and `get_pitch_notes` (see
docs/AI_ASSISTANTS.md).

Screenshot: `screenshots/app-pitch-editor.png` in the project files.

## On the PC

- [ ] **Find the notes of a real vocal.** Use a sung phrase, such as
      the hook from song test 2. Pass: one blob per sung note. Vibrato
      doesn't split a note, and two of the same note in a row are two
      blobs. Note any phrase where it cuts badly: fast runs, breathy
      notes and slides are the hard cases.
- [ ] **Move one note.** Drag a note up two semitones and play it.
      Pass: only that note moves, with no click at either end, and it
      still sounds like the same singer.
- [ ] **Snap to notes.** On a phrase sung a little out of tune, press
      Snap to notes. Pass: it sounds in tune without sounding robotic,
      because drift and vibrato are kept.
- [ ] **Flatten drift, take out vibrato.** Set Drift kept to 0 on a
      note that wanders, and Vibrato to 0 on one with vibrato. Pass: the
      first holds steady and the second goes straight. Try Vibrato at
      200 %: a wider vibrato at the same speed.
- [ ] **Formants.** Move one note's formants down 3 st and then up 3 st.
      Pass: bigger and then smaller in character, at the same pitch.
      Decide whether that is useful enough to keep in the bar.
- [ ] **Time.** Drag a note later, and drag a note's end to lengthen it.
      Pass: it moves and the audio around it closes up naturally. Check
      for smearing at the note's start: the stretcher's attack handling
      has only been tested on plucked notes.
- [ ] **A guitar line.** Find the notes of a single-note guitar or bass
      line and bend one note. Pass: usable, although a plucked note's
      attack may smear a little.
- [ ] **Undo, save, reopen.** Edit, undo, redo, save and reopen. Pass:
      the edits are kept, and the faint pitch line comes back a moment
      after the project opens.
