# 6.1 Audio that follows tempo

Audio clips can be set to musical time: they keep to the song's beats,
stretched to the tempo map and the project tempo as it plays and when
exporting. Stretch markers tie a frame of the audio to a place on the
timeline; they are found from attacks or placed by hand, and dragged
onto beats to move an attack with no cuts, gaps or doubled attacks.
Audio quantise moves every attack toward the grid by a strength, within
a window. And a tempo map can be made from a free performance, so the
song follows the player.

Everything is in the Edit menu and the clip's right-click menu, and
every command runs through the shared command table: `follow-tempo`,
`clip-tempo`, `stretch-markers-from-attacks`, `add-stretch-marker`,
`clear-stretch-markers`, `quantise-audio` (window) and
`quantise-audio-apply`, `tempo-from-clip` (window) and
`tempo-from-clip-apply`. Agents see a clip's `tempo`, `beat` and
`markers` in `get_project`.

## On the PC

- [ ] **6.1 Following tempo.** Import a loop or record a bar or two at
      the song's tempo. Right-click the clip and tick **Follow tempo**.
      Change the song's tempo (the tempo field, or a tempo change at a
      bar). Pass: the clip stretches to the new tempo and stays on its
      bar, in pitch; its name shows the tempo it was played at (♩120).
      Export the song: the exported audio matches what plays.
- [ ] **6.1 A loop at another tempo.** Import a loop played at a tempo
      that isn't the song's. Right-click → **Clip tempo…** and press
      **Guess from length** (or type the loop's tempo), then **Set
      tempo**. Pass: the loop plays in time with the metronome, and its
      length on the timeline is whole bars.
- [ ] **6.1 Stretch markers.** On a loose take, choose **Stretch markers
      from attacks**. Pass: a flag at the top of the clip at each
      attack. Drag one onto a beat line (snapping on). Pass: the attack
      plays on the beat, the audio either side is stretched evenly, and
      there is no click, gap or doubled attack. Undo puts it back in one
      step. Right-click a marker → **Delete stretch marker** removes it.
- [ ] **6.1 Quantise audio.** Select the take, **Quantise audio…**,
      half a beat, 100%, 60 ms. Pass: every attack within 60 ms of a
      grid line lands on it and the take sounds tight; those further off
      stay put. At 50% they move half way, so it still sounds played.
- [ ] **6.1 Grouped tracks.** Two tracks in an edit group (a close and a
      room mic of the same part): select the close mic's take and
      quantise, or drag one of its markers. Pass: the room mic moves by
      the same amounts at the same places, and the two stay in phase.
- [ ] **6.1 Tempo from a performance.** Record a few bars in free time,
      with a clear beat. Select the take, **Tempo from performance…**,
      "A beat", and press **Set the song's tempo**. Pass: the song's bar
      lines land on your beats, the metronome follows you, and the
      take itself is not stretched. If you started right at the song's
      start, the take moves one bar later to leave a count-in.
- [ ] **6.1 Sound of the stretch.** Listen to a clip following a tempo
      a lot slower and a lot faster than it was played (say 70% and
      140%). Note how much it smears or flutters: this decides the
      speed/pitch library (0.6).

## Decisions to accept

- [x] **Built on our own stretcher, at risk.** The speed and pitch
      library is still your choice (0.6 listening). The warp code talks
      to the stretcher through one small interface (`warp::Stretch`:
      set, reset, process, plus "play along this map"), so a library can
      replace our phase vocoder behind it without touching the clips or
      the timeline. If you pick a library that only takes a speed, it
      follows the map block by block instead of frame by frame.
- [x] **Attacks land within about 4 ms.** Tests put a tied frame within
      4 ms of where it was asked for at stretches of ±25%, and exactly
      at 1:1. A phase vocoder spreads an attack over part of a frame, so
      the loudest point sits a few milliseconds off when the stretch
      changes at that attack. Say if that is audible when you try
      quantising a take; a sharper transient treatment would be the next
      step.
- [x] **A clip's tempo is the tempo in force where it starts.** Turning
      Follow tempo on takes the song's tempo there as the clip's own, so
      nothing changes until the tempo does. Clip tempo… sets it by hand
      for loops played at another tempo.
- [x] **Clips that follow tempo move with the tempo map.** After a
      tempo change they start where their beat now falls, as notes do.
      Moving or trimming a clip sets its beat from where it is.
- [x] **A tempo map from a performance puts a tempo change on every
      beat** (or every bar, when told each attack is a bar), as plain
      jumps, replacing the tempo changes the song had. Tempo changes
      are on whole beats, so half-beat performances aren't offered.
      The lead-in before the first beat is a whole number of beats at
      a tempo chosen to fit it; a take that starts within half a beat
      of the song's start is moved one bar later instead.
- [x] **No preview for audio quantise.** Fix timing shows what it will
      do; Quantise audio just does it, and one undo step takes it back.
      Say if the preview is missed.
- [x] **Markers stay with their audio.** Splitting, trimming and
      slipping a clip keep each marker on the frame of audio it marks;
      fades and the volume line are by frames of audio too, so they
      stretch with it.
- [x] **Markers can't stretch more than 3× against the clip's tempo**,
      either way, and the stretcher takes ¼× to 4× in all. A clip
      whose markers or tempo ask for more plays plainly until they are
      fixed (no warning yet).
